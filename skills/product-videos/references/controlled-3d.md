# Direct and finish a 3D product film

Use 3D when the film needs control over the object's form, physical action, materials, lighting or camera. Develop the product argument first. Rotation can reveal form; demonstrating a useful consequence needs an action or result the viewer can understand. Decide what each passage reveals and what the render must make visible.

## Build the asset for the intended views

Keep one coherent master product with meaningful parts, real proportions and explicit pivots. For a fictional product, establish its construction and behavior as design decisions before animating it. For an existing product, verify them against supplied evidence. Do not invent internal parts merely because an exploded view is available.

Judge detail at the closest planned view. Edge radii, seams, thickness, contact surfaces, markings and normal continuity can determine whether the object reads as manufactured. Give different materials distinct roughness and reflection behavior at a credible texture scale. Check a diffuser, coating or translucent shell with its actual thickness and light path; an attractive opaque substitute may change the demonstrated product.

Separate product action, editorial camera and lighting controls. An articulated head needs a stable pivot and clearance throughout its travel. If it emits light, its source should follow the same orientation and intensity state as the visible emitter. Keep studio lights separate so a change intended to demonstrate product behavior is not accidentally supplied by the surrounding lighting.

Establish what initiates a physical action when that distinction affects the claim. An unaccompanied moving part can imply automation even when copy invites manual adjustment. A hand, visible control or another convincing cause may be needed; attractive motion and imperative wording alone do not prove how the product operates.

## Compose the light and camera together

Shape reflections to describe the form. Source size, placement and contrast should make planes, curves and edges readable; adding more lights can flatten those relationships. Inspect dark surfaces and bright emitters together. A white patch without surface detail may be an exposure or reflection problem rather than insufficient samples.

Choose the lens, distance and focus for the information in the shot. A macro view can establish a tactile detail before a wider image restores context. Depth of field should guide attention while leaving the mechanism or evidence legible. Check the combined camera and object path for unintended crops, collisions and changes in apparent scale. Check the set's coverage throughout that path too: widening a background does not repair an exposed edge in the surface beneath it.

Render a representative image of the actual product early, including the most demanding material or lighting relationship. Inspect it at delivery scale before extending the look across the film.

## Prove motion before the full render

Build a complete low-cost assembly to judge the order of discoveries, action-to-result links and cumulative pauses. Use the intended delivery cadence. A viewport preview or simpler shading can help develop the edit, but cannot establish the final material or motion-blur behavior.

Then render a contiguous passage containing a consequential acceleration, contact, pivot or lighting change with the intended finishing settings. Check intermediate geometry and the leading subject's screen motion. Judge shutter blur against the travel and the readable arrival; neither blur nor slow playback repairs weak choreography.

Track the product's visible consequence as well as its mechanism. A lamp head's peak angular speed may occur before or after the fastest travel of its light pool across a page. Judge the curve by the image carrying the meaning, including projection and camera movement. Give that result enough exposure to be understood, then cut when its contribution is complete. See the [Aster example](original-film-decisions.md#let-a-physical-products-output-lead-the-film).

Inspect neighboring frames for unstable reflections, crawling texture, shadow noise and denoising changes. A clean still can conceal instability over time. Compare raw and denoised versions when filtering may be creating blotches or erasing material detail. Correct the cause in the geometry, light, exposure, sampling or filtering before increasing every quality setting together.

## Render and assemble without losing the decisions

Benchmark representative scene frames. Separate initial setup or shader compilation from repeated rendering cost. Choose sample count and resolution by inspecting their effect at the intended viewing scale.

Use a viewport or browser preview when faster feedback helps choose camera, light direction or timing. Save the approved values and verify their translation into the final renderer. A live-scene connector improves access; a browser path tracer changes the rendering route. Neither establishes parity with the final materials, sampling, color or motion blur without a representative comparison.

Keep numbered image sequences when they make expensive rendering resumable and shot revisions local. Preserve the frame origin, cadence, camera state and output settings. Check the renderer's process lifetime before starting a long batch, and confirm its exit status and frame inventory before assembly. Keep deliberate holds distinct from accidentally duplicated moving frames.

Reuse a rendered plate for a deliberate hold only when the complete visible scene is unchanged, including camera, focus, lights and procedural effects. Choose a fully settled frame whose shutter interval excludes the preceding motion, and record the reuse in the assembly. Preserve every intended frame of motion. A scene or lighting repair invalidates affected cached frames even if their filenames still exist.

Choose color management before final lighting and compositing. Record whether a plate is scene-linear or already carries its display transform, and verify a representative round trip through the finishing tool. Applying a display transform twice or treating a linear plate as display-ready can change contrast and color even when the file decodes correctly.

Retain the editable scene, animation controls, material setup, render settings and required asset references alongside the final film. Verify that the saved scene can find its dependencies. Keep typography and sound editable where the intended revisions need that control. Deliver the complete playable sequence and distinguish rendered-image checks, motion inspection, listening and the user's creative acceptance.
