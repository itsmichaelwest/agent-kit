# Product videos

Give the product a distinctive, convincing presence through composition, performance, motion, editing and sound. Develop options the user can review, produce the film and diagnose weaknesses. The user makes the final creative decisions.

Preserve the user's approved direction. When a material creative choice remains open, give the user something concrete to compare and continue authorized production between decisions. Treat approval as specific to the version reviewed. A successful export confirms technical delivery; judge creative quality separately.

## Own the film

Use this guide for temporal storytelling, shot construction, motion, sound, assembly and film review. For an original product film or a story revision, use `product-storytelling` alongside it when available: establish the argument, give words and images distinct jobs, and apply its subtraction pass to the complete sequence. Even a short script needs that editorial work. For a bounded motion repair, carry the approved story forward without reopening it. Use `product-web-design` for a surrounding marketing website; it does not set the film's shot structure. Use the relevant production-tool skill for implementation, and let the treatment decide which of its effects to use.

For authored UI shots, use `stageshow` when available for believable components, purposeful framing and connected action-to-result motion. Keep this guide responsible for the film's cadence, expressive ambition, edits and sound. Adapt website-demo timing to the shot; verify the capture path before relying on it for video. See [Authored UI](references/production.md#construct-authored-ui-for-the-shot).

A quiet demonstration, an expressive typographic passage, a photographed performance and a precisely lit object can all lead. Choose from what this product lets the audience discover. The guidance does not prescribe a palette, font, shot count, duration, type treatment or fixed narrative arc.

For an original film, transfer a reference's craft without inheriting its recognizable treatment. Develop the visual mechanism from the new product, and review originality separately from motion quality; see [Direction and story](references/direction.md#transfer-craft-without-inheriting-the-treatment).

For branded product films, plan a clean product-name or logo end card by default. Resolve the product story before that final identification; see [Closing the film](references/direction.md#resolve-the-story-and-close-on-the-brand).

## Develop the film through reviewable work

1. Establish the audience, product truth, intended feeling and delivery context from the brief and existing decisions. Identify what must remain exact and what is a visual interpretation. For scene purpose, reference analysis and editorial development, read [Direction and story](references/direction.md).
2. Compose the consequential images and the change connecting them. Decide what leads attention, what persists and what becomes different. Establish the cadence of understanding before timing individual objects. Read [Motion construction](references/motion.md) for phrase-level timing, purposeful edits, acceleration, typography, shared geometry and product actions. Build the passage with the greatest unresolved creative or production risk before extending the film.
3. Choose tools by the control a shot and its likely revisions need. Read [Production methods](references/production.md) for footage, authored UI, 3D, generative media, compositing and editable assembly. Use [Tool selection](references/tool-selection.md) when choosing or changing a production route. Inspect the available assets and verify that the tools are installed and connected.
4. Develop picture and sound together. Read [Music and sound](references/music-and-sound.md) when choosing music, designing cues, analyzing audio or mixing. Present meaningful options when taste remains unresolved; preserve an established musical choice.
5. Review and revise the actual sequence. Read [Film review](references/review.md) before declaring a version ready. Compare against the commissioned qualities, diagnose the cause of a shortfall and repair that cause. Keep creative assessment, technical checks and user acceptance separate.

Scale the process to the task. A timing repair may need only a few frames and the surrounding passage; a new film needs a coherent assembly. Complete the authorized deliverable, including normal-speed playback and editable sources. Treatments and contact sheets support production. Put material limitations, implementation notes and unresolved production logistics in the handoff, outside the public story.

## Preserve useful freedom

Translate words such as “snappy,” “expressive” and “cinematic” into choices that can be constructed: visible acceleration, changing letter proportions, a shared edge, an interrupted rhythm, a lighting change or a particular sound relationship. Judge their combined effect in the film; matching numerical targets or adding effects alone cannot establish craft.

For independent letter width, weight and spacing, use the [original variable-type construction](references/variable-type-example.md). Choose font controls or prepared outlines supported by the production environment; an optional TypeScript helper handles sampled outlines. Label what was rendered, measured, heard and accepted. Example values belong to that example unless a real technical constraint makes them necessary. Keep source-specific reconstructions and restricted reference media outside the reusable skill; they can inform a commissioned study without becoming hidden dependencies.

For original 2D and 3D examples of product behavior shaping picture and sound, read [Original film decisions](references/original-film-decisions.md). Adapt the successful relationships within the scope of the recorded feedback. Choose palettes, timings and shot orders for the new film.
