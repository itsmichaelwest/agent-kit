# Choose and direct music and sound

## Define the musical job

Decide what music contributes to this film: intimacy, anticipation, propulsion, humor, release, a cultural association or continuity between different images. Use the user's existing references and preferences. Ask about a missing preference only when it materially changes the direction, and continue independent picture work while it remains open.

Separate mood, tempo, rhythmic density, instrumentation, vocal presence and development. Beats per minute cannot tell whether an arrangement leaves room for speech, whether a lyric changes the product's meaning or whether the ending resolves the film. Identify the actual passages that need space, change, emphasis or continuity.

Search named artists and recordings as well as production libraries, assessing fit and licensing for each. A library track can carry a distinctive musical identity; a familiar artist can be a poor fit. Search for the role and passage the film needs, using genre tags as one clue.

## Prepare an audition the user can judge

When the direction is unresolved, make a small shortlist with a recommendation and genuinely different readings of the picture. Preserve an accepted choice instead of restarting the search without a reason. For each candidate, retain:

- Track title, artist, exact recording or version, and a verified listening link.
- The intended role and proposed excerpt. Give timestamps only after inspecting that version.
- The meaningful comparison: vocal meaning, entry, development, dialogue space, contrast or ending.
- Known instrumental versions, alternates or stems, with availability verified for that track.
- Whether the agent heard it, inspected only metadata or is using the user's description.

Assess musical fit through listening. If only tags or waveform data are available, prepare a playable comparison and state the listening limitation in the handoff. The user decides whether the sound feels right.

Label each track's production status: reference only, temporary edit music, or intended release track. Verify usage rights separately and keep temporary choices replaceable. Before making a distinctive lyric, break or ending indispensable, establish that the selected version is a viable production choice.

## Let picture and music develop together

Audition a representative picture passage at comparable playback levels. Use actual dialogue where available and identify a temporary voice in production notes. A music-only comparison helps isolate the track's effect; an edited comparison can reveal a stronger relationship. State which changed so the user can judge the cause.

Plan around phrases, arrangement changes, breaths, tension and release as well as beats. A cut may land on an accent, an action may lead it, or a shot may cross several beats to finish its meaning. Music can connect visibly different shots. Neither every cut nor every animated element needs a sound hit.

Keep cue edits reversible. Record source version, excerpt ranges, joins, fades and important anchors. Listen through edits, lyrical joins and the ending. An automatic duration fit may produce a technically continuous file while damaging phrasing or meaning. When replacing a preview with a release file, compare alignment and content again; filenames alone do not establish identical audio.

Use available stems when separate musical groups would help the edit, preserving the full mix as a reference. Arrangement, instrument choice and cue placement can create space for speech before level reduction is needed. Keep the musical development audible rather than treating the track as a constant background bed by default.

## Design a coherent sound palette

Choose layers for their contribution: authentic product feedback, contact or material detail, location ambience, designed emphasis, musical gesture and silence. Distinguish an actual product sound from an expressive film effect when checking the promise. An impact should not accidentally imply a mechanism or feedback behavior the product lacks.

A recurring family of sounds can establish continuity. Vary size, spectrum, duration and distance with each event instead of attaching the same whoosh to every movement. A large visual action can work with a quiet detail or deliberate silence when that contrast serves the direction.

Map a cue's onset, strongest accent, duration and decay against the visual onset, fastest travel, contact and settle. The sound's perceived moment may differ from the start of its file. Audition offsets in context; a fixed frame lead or lag is not correct for every cue.

For a tightly authored sequence, let picture and sound read the same event ledger for edits, contacts and state changes. If the soundtrack is itself product output, use the shared product model described in [Product truth during motion](motion.md#preserve-product-truth-during-motion). A common time source prevents accidental disagreement; it does not determine whether a cue should lead, coincide with or follow the visible action.

A synthesized or generated palette can be a finished artistic choice or a provisional sketch. Judge the actual result. Identify provisional sound in the handoff, keep its sources and controls, and do not equate clipping checks with musical quality.

## Use sonic analysis as evidence

Waveforms locate changes in amplitude, candidate attacks and silence. Onset strength and spectral change can reveal rhythmic or timbral events. Energy envelopes help compare a buildup with a release. These analyses can suggest edit points; they do not determine which accent matters musically or emotionally.

Inspect the relevant passage in detail, going beyond a whole-track beat grid to distinguish a sustained bass note from a percussive attack and an incidental transient from a phrase change. Compare detected events with actual listening and picture. Keep the analysis aligned to the exact source version, trim and sample rate used in the edit.

Use an analysis library such as [librosa](https://librosa.org/doc/latest/index.html) when candidate onsets, beats or energy changes would help build that map. For a recorded voice, [WhisperX](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX) can propose word timings; verify product names, numbers and timing-critical words against the actual recording. Neither tool chooses a suitable track, interprets a performance or establishes that an accent belongs in the edit.

Keep detected landmarks separate from authored cues. Record source time, edit time, the associated visual event and whether the relationship anticipates, coincides with or follows the sound. Use waveform or word timing to narrow the offsets to audition in the picture. After a trim, speed change or replacement recording, update the mapping before trusting it again.

Technical mix checks include clipping, unexpected gaps, channel behavior, synchronization and the requested delivery loudness. Choose targets for the actual destination; do not apply a universal loudness value to every film. Listen at normal speed, including quiet and dense passages, on relevant everyday playback as well as headphones when available. Report any untested listening conditions.

## Resolve rights for the selected use

Verify current permissions for the selected recording, library plan and intended delivery. A general label such as “royalty-free” or a catalog listing cannot establish clearance. For an existing commercial recording, investigate the applicable composition and recording permissions. For a library asset, verify the actual project, client, advertising, channel, territory and term requirements.

Keep the source, license or permission record and usage scope with production notes. Check whether delivering an editable project with source audio has different conditions from delivering a rendered film. Do not redistribute standalone music or sound assets merely because they were used in a video. Apply the user's existing authorization to purchases, uploads, contacts and publication; the skill itself authorizes none of them.

## Keep the mix revisable

Retain independent dialogue, music, effects and ambience where those layers exist. Name cues by the event they support. After a picture change, inspect affected synchronization, reading space and musical joins; after a track change, inspect whether the whole edit still develops properly.

Present the complete mix for the user to judge its feeling as well as its technical quality. Preserve the chosen version and unresolved delivery conditions. File playback, waveform and level checks cover only the technical part of that review.
