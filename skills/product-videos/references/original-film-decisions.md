# Learn from original film decisions

These original fictional-product studies show how product behavior can shape composition, movement and sound. Alcove developed through guided revisions. Lilt and Tessera tested the portable guides on new subjects, and Aster extended the work into controlled 3D. Adapt the decisions and their consequences, choosing the palette, font, timing and shot order for the new film.

## Give one product different motion languages

Alcove is a planning app that makes room for focused work. In one treatment, a selected calendar interval expands into a reservation surface, and its fill becomes the field for the next typographic phrase. The product action supplies a shared boundary and a visual connection to the benefit.

A second treatment gives the same story a faster rhythm through normal-proportion lettering: complete-word replacements, stationary additions and removals, brief contractions into alignment, stepped reframing and accelerating exits. The selected hour receives more exposure than the rejected gaps because it is the useful result. Expressive typography does not require deforming every word; editing, hierarchy and asymmetric exposure can carry the emphasis.

Removing a second emphasis on “Make” released time for product evidence without weakening the energetic motion. The close became a clean product-name card after the story resolved. In a new film, judge how much exposure the result needs and remove emphasis that repeats an understood point. Choose the devices that serve that treatment; these alternatives need not be combined.

## Let the soundtrack demonstrate a music product

Lilt is a music sketchpad. A tapped beat becomes a loop; bass and melody join; muting and restoring bass demonstrates control. The soundtrack is the sketch being built, so the result can be heard as well as seen.

As the arrangement grows, new lanes open above existing ones and change the space available for lettering. A large compressed instrument name contracts into its lane label; melody letters land at the corresponding notes' step and pitch before becoming a label. The type follows the product's musical structure.

A shared song model supplies visible notes, playback flashes and synthesized audio. During mute, the bass notes remain as outlines, stop flashing and become silent while the other parts continue. Input ripples identify the recording pass; playback does not pretend that someone taps the notes again. Camera and editorial type timings remain separate from the song data. The close identifies Lilt with a wordmark whose dot recalls a sequencer cell.

Use several media to express the same event when they help communicate the product's behavior. An audio demonstration needs audible differences; another product may need a spatial or numerical consequence.

The author's review identified an opening that waits on an empty grid, additional layers arriving without visible entry and pauses that might read as waiting rather than listening. These remain revision candidates from the author's review; the user did not identify particular shots. A revision could begin with a meaningful tap, show how a layer enters and keep only the listening time needed to understand the loop and mute. Preserve the musical proof while editing its preparation.

## Let product geometry direct the lettering

Tessera adapts one event poster into portrait, square and banner formats while preserving its words and identity. Format controls visibly initiate the changes; a later drag demonstrates that the designer can adjust the offered variation.

The artboard's current edges determine the headline's available width. A solver selects variable-font letter proportions for that width; weight can continue changing after width arrives. The information row spans the headline block, and the artwork is recomposed within the same moving artboard, so the composition changes as one related design. [Independent letter controls](variable-type-example.md) let the letterforms adapt within the poster without scaling the whole poster.

During one reshape, a delayed footer crossed the moving artboard edge. The author's checks of isolated transition frames missed it; an outside review of consecutive frames exposed it. The repair anchored both footer and neighboring headline to the current bottom edge, interpolating their margins instead of unrelated absolute positions. Check neighboring elements too when repairing a shared boundary; the correction can introduce a new collision.

The completed formats assemble into a tile arrangement that becomes the product mark; the name then closes the film. The result supplies the ending's visual connection to the product. That relationship is useful when it arises naturally; another film can close decisively with a simple cut to its name.

The author's review flagged accumulated stillness across the comparison and brand close. A tighter version would judge what the viewer needs to compare, resolve that thought and place the musical cadence accordingly. Adding drift would not answer whether the pauses are needed. The colors, font, format count and durations belong to this example.

## Let a physical product's output lead the film

Aster is an adjustable task lamp, and the film follows its pool of light. A tilt brings light onto a page, a closer view reveals a fine adjustment, and turning the collar dims the illumination. The wider resolved workspace then shows what those controls make possible. The object, its light and the surrounding space each get their own view.

The lamp has a coherent master model with explicit pivots. The visible emitter and the light cast onto the desk follow the same product state, while studio lighting remains separately controlled. Music and effects read the edit's event times; typography stays editable over the rendered plates. This extends Lilt's shared-event principle into a physical scene without reproducing its visual treatment.

Judge the leading image's movement. The head's rotation and the light pool's travel across the frame have different speed profiles because of projection and their spatial relationship. A pleasing angular curve does not alone establish a convincing arrival on the page.

The edit shortened from 19.5 to 14 seconds by removing waiting and exhausted holds while preserving the product actions' acceleration and braking. Check what can be tightened between discoveries before changing motion curves that already work. The resulting duration belongs to this study; a longer commissioned film needs further earned development.

One limitation remains useful: movement and imperative copy suggested manual adjustment, but no hand established it unambiguously. When manual versus automatic operation affects the promise, show a convincing cause rather than relying on wording to resolve it.

## Keep the evidence bounded

The user approved Alcove's later motion direction, judged Lilt and Tessera good baselines with tighter motion and storytelling still desired, and subsequently referred positively to the recent 2D and 3D outputs. Preserve those approaches without interpreting broad feedback as approval of every shot or mix.

The studies were rendered and checked through decoded frames, consecutive motion frames, product-state comparisons and audio measurements. Agent reviews did not establish normal-speed perception or listening quality. Tessera includes a review-assisted geometry repair; Aster includes production and edit revisions. Neither should be described as an unassisted first-pass result. The examples explain produced work, but reading them does not validate a new film or guarantee the same quality.
