# Direct the product story

## Find the film's proposition

State what changes for the viewer: a useful discovery, a belief supported by evidence, an emotional response, or anticipation of an experience. Identify the product fact that gives the proposition substance. A feature list is raw material; choose what deserves sustained attention and what can be understood in a glimpse.

Develop a visual premise from the product's form, behavior or consequence. A small detail might become a whole environment, or competing tasks might give way to one clear action. Words can also make physical room for an image. Explore relationships that suit the product; the plot remains open. Describe what becomes visible and why that change belongs to this product.

Choose words, image and sound together. They can carry different parts of the thought. Copy may express a human benefit while a screen proves the action; a material detail may invite attention before an explanation. Remove repeated narration of something already clear unless repetition supplies rhythm, recognition or closure.

Preserve the difference between a demonstration and a metaphor. A camera can travel through a screen to emphasize a result without claiming that the application performs that zoom. A stylized material transformation can express lightness without proving a measured weight. Keep factual conditions that affect the promise; put prototype and production details in the handoff.

## Read references as directed events

Inspect the actual supplied film. A broad contact sheet helps locate a passage; it cannot establish acceleration, overlap, editorial rhythm or sound. Study the relevant sequence at its native cadence and listen where that capability is available. Record observation separately from inference about how the film was made.

For a useful passage, identify:

- The discovery or feeling, and what the audience knows beforehand.
- The leading subject, its scale, crop and relationship to the frame.
- The change in composition: which parts persist, replace, expand, yield or become hidden.
- The onset, fastest travel, recognition, arrival and any residual movement.
- The next event's relationship to the current one: overlap, interruption, response or pause.
- The musical phrase, accent, silence or sound texture, and what it contributes.

If matching a passage is the task, retain exact frame identity and time origin. If originating a film is the task, extract the relationships that matter and design new content around them. Similar colors, a familiar font or fast cuts alone do not establish comparable direction. Do not infer a production method merely from a convincing final image.

## Transfer craft without inheriting the treatment

Separate what makes a reference work from what makes it recognizable. Its decisive acceleration, clear hierarchy, overlap, compositional continuity and contrast between action and recognition can inform another film. Its particular lettering, material, silhouette, framing and order of reveals belong to that treatment. Changing the words or palette can leave the same underlying sequence intact.

For an original brief, begin with the new product's own behavior or consequence and give it a visual mechanism. Describe the event without naming the reference: what acts, what resists or responds, what remains, and what the audience discovers. Then apply the desired motion qualities to that event. For example, protecting time could become a space that remains undisturbed while a conflicting request finds an alternative. That premise permits many treatments and needs no particular font, metaphor or animation tool.

Compare the important images and their progression with the reference. If the leading silhouette, subject relationships and reveal order still identify the source, reconsider the premise or choreography before polishing surface differences. Compare the intended energy too, because originality and craft are separate review questions: pursuing a different visual identity must not quietly replace an expressive brief with a restrained one.

Shared techniques, colors, type styles and familiar film conventions remain available. Label a commissioned reconstruction when it deliberately retains the source treatment. For an original film, preserve the user's approved motion qualities while developing a direction that belongs to its subject. Approval of a timing exercise applies to its timing; the visual identity still needs review.

## Compose the important states

Design the entry, meaningful change and result when they reveal the passage's construction. These may occur within one shot or across cuts. Establish dominant subject, available space, silhouettes, alignment, depth, important words and the path of attention. Use real copy and representative product content; placeholder length can hide an impossible composition.

Make the opening image contribute before its animation finishes. It can establish the product, an intriguing detail or an action already underway. An empty interface waiting for its first useful state needs a deliberate reason; a camera settle alone does not introduce the proposition.

Connect a demonstrated action to its result. If a new layer, variation or feature appears, decide whether its cause is already understood, shown through an input, or intentionally omitted. An ellipsis can compress familiar work; it weakens the demonstration when it hides the capability being promised. Show enough of the causal link without turning every passage into a tutorial.

Give the viewer something to do during a hold: read, compare, listen, anticipate or inspect a material. Judge whether that task continues after the image becomes clear, then cut, overlap the next event or retain the pause. Remove drift once the image has exhausted its purpose, and keep the time needed for a necessary explanation.

Let a change in framing serve a change in attention. A detail can fill the frame while the whole product remains understood; a wider view can restore context after an intimate action. A persistent full application window is useful when context matters and wasteful when it makes the meaningful action too small.

## Develop rhythm across the assembly

Consider how passages relate. Accumulation can build pressure that a quick action releases. A plain sentence can make the next expressive passage feel more forceful. Continuous motion can explain shared space, while a cut can introduce a new thought with greater clarity. Choose the transition for that relationship.

Repetition may establish a motif or make comparisons fair. Change the treatment when the next idea needs different attention. If every passage performs an entrance, pauses and exits, identify whether the film is developing its proposition or merely presenting more cards. Replacing all durations with faster values rarely repairs an undeveloped edit.

Keep the user's feedback attached to a version and passage. Separate an objection to the idea from an objection to its execution. If the visual premise fails, offer a materially different treatment; if the premise works but the arrival feels weak, repair the motion. Avoid reopening accepted product facts or visual direction for a routine adjustment.

## Resolve the story and close on the brand

For a branded product film, use a product name, wordmark or logo as the default final image. Let the preceding passage complete the demonstration, emotional payoff or product reveal, then give the identity a clear moment of its own. The last frame should leave the viewer with a recognizable product or brand.

Compose the end card deliberately: mark or name, scale, surrounding space, background, arrival, hold and musical resolution. A simple cut and a still mark can provide a decisive ending after an energetic sequence. Use the supplied identity accurately and hold it long enough to register at the intended viewing size; no fixed duration or logo animation is required.

Review the comparison, payoff and brand hold as one ending. Several individually plausible pauses can flatten the final passage together. Place the musical cadence where the story resolves. If an image has finished its work, consider shortening or rephrasing the music instead of waiting for another bar. Keep a long brand hold when the brief, placement or intended feeling gives it a purpose.

Add a tagline, offer, URL or call to action when the brief gives it a job. Otherwise, let the name or mark carry the card. An intentional story-led ending or required campaign format can depart from this default. Choose that departure explicitly while preserving clear attribution.

## Build the consequential uncertainty first

Choose a short passage that contains the hardest promised capability: expressive lettering, exact UI action, physical contact, a material reveal, a difficult transition or sound-led development. Make its important states and a playable construction. The test must exercise the capability; a camera move around fixed type does not test letterform choreography.

Evaluate it against the requested qualities before scaling the approach across the film. A small copy or geometry change can expose whether the method is revisable. It does not establish artistic acceptance or general applicability. Carry demonstrated construction details into the working guidance; keep proposals labeled until produced and reviewed.
