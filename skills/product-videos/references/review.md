# Review the film and repair the cause

## Judge the sequence before its measurements

Review at the intended size and normal playback speed with the relevant sound. Ask what the viewer notices, understands and feels as the sequence develops. Then use adjacent frames, slower playback and measurements to explain a weakness or check a fragile boundary.

Describe what the review method lets you assess. Browser playback status confirms that a video plays; contact sheets show sampled compositions, and waveforms show signal changes. Assessing rhythm requires continuous-motion perception, and assessing sound requires listening. State any limits in the review and provide the user a playable result.

## Separate the review questions

| Dimension | Useful questions | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story | Does the film develop a product proposition? What changes in understanding? Does a passage repeat a resolved idea? | The complete assembly, copy and product evidence. |
| Direction and craft | Is attention deliberate? Do composition, typography, material detail, timing and sound achieve the requested character? | Normal-speed review, important poses and equivalent reference passages when supplied. |
| Original direction, when commissioned | Does the visual mechanism belong to this product? Do the main images and their progression have an identity beyond changed copy and styling? | The authored premise and sequence, compared with reference-specific silhouettes, relationships and reveal order. |
| Motion construction | Are acceleration, travel, braking, overlap and contact what was intended? Does type actually change the properties the treatment promises? | Consecutive frames, geometry and relevant velocity or scale-ratio samples. |
| Product fidelity | Do identity, geometry, states, numbers and claimed results remain coherent? | Approved product material and before/during/after states. |
| Sound | Do music, silence and cues develop the picture? Are speech, joins and emphasis clear? | Listening to the actual mix and source version; technical analysis as support. |
| Delivery | Does the exported file contain the expected picture, audio, cadence and duration? Can it be played in the requested context? | Decode and metadata checks, inspection of the delivered file. |
| Creative acceptance | Does this version satisfy the user's taste and intended direction? | The user's feedback on that version. |

Assess each dimension separately. Correct masks and readable labels can coexist with unconvincing direction, and a compelling excerpt can sit within a weak edit. Record the user's acceptance separately from the agent's technical review.

Apply the storytelling subtraction pass to the actual picture and sound. Identify what each headline, number, label, icon, cue and repeated image adds. Remove a redundant explanation without automatically replacing it with decoration. Keep repetition that earns its place through recognition, contrast or closure. Simplification should strengthen the leading image and its development while keeping the requested expressive ambition.

Record partial acceptance precisely. If the user approves the motion but rejects its resemblance to a reference, preserve the approved acceleration, timing relationships and clarity while changing the visual premise and choreography. Do not mark the whole film accepted or discard the successful motion work.

A good-baseline judgment accepts a direction worth developing. It does not accept every shot, the mix or finished production quality. Preserve what worked and keep the requested refinement open. Broad feedback that motion and storytelling could be tighter does not identify a particular shot, so investigate opening delays, unclear causes, redundant passages and accumulated holds as possible causes.

## Compare the qualities that matter

For supplied references, compare at equivalent viewing scale and intended cadence. Identify the defining relationships: image and type scale, compositional change, speed and braking, rhythm of actions and holds, surface detail, continuity and musical development. Evaluate the original film against those qualities; it need not copy the source's wording or every shot.

For a direct reconstruction, align exact source and output exposures before claiming correspondence. Retain source time origin and presentation timestamps where relevant. Explain any cadence conversion; equal nominal frame rate and duration can hide duplicated or shifted frames. Keep source-specific measurements in the commissioned research, outside reusable guidance.

Without a supplied reference, assess the approved brief, product argument and authored craft goals. Do not invent a benchmark after seeing the result or silently lower a stated one because a render completed.

## Diagnose before revising

Tie feedback to a passage and version. Distinguish the kind of shortfall before choosing a repair:

- If the idea does not develop, change the editorial proposition, relationship or sequence.
- If important evidence is too small or unclear, change framing, hierarchy or the demonstrated state.
- If a move feels flat, inspect visible travel, acceleration, arrival and the next event's onset.
- If type feels ordinary despite an expressive brief, inspect glyph design, spacing, shared space and compositional change before adding another whole-frame zoom.
- If a transition feels disconnected, inspect the intended visual or sonic relationship across the boundary.
- If the sequence lacks energy, check musical development, temporal contrast and empty waiting as well as raw speed.
- If the result feels like a reskinned reference, revisit the product-specific visual mechanism, dominant images and their progression. Changing colors or copy alone may preserve the problem.

Investigate these possible causes before choosing a repair. Still type can suit a restrained film, and a camera move can be the right central event. Preserve the parts the user has approved. Repair a rejected experiment's specific failure and keep the technique available.

For a tightening pass, first locate the first meaningful image, each action-to-result link, the next new discovery and the last necessary comparison. Check those narrative boundaries and the purpose of the holds before changing easing constants. Then refine acceleration, overlap and arrival within the stronger edit. Recheck sound and product state after retiming; a faster sequence can still explain the wrong thing or leave its musical ending behind.

## Make creative comparisons easy to direct

When a choice needs iteration, expose the few controls that affect it: attack and braking, event overlap, type width or tracking, camera framing, light direction, or sound offset. A small review panel such as [Tweakpane](https://github.com/cocopon/tweakpane), an editor's native controls or two rendered alternatives can all work. Label controls by the visible or audible decision. Save the chosen values into the same configuration used for export.

Hold unrelated variables constant when diagnosing a specific weakness. For an open treatment choice, offer distinct readings of the same product action: plain and snappy motion can use stable letterforms and decisive substitutions; an expressive version can reshape shared space and lettering. Both need authored acceleration, hierarchy and a reason for each pause. Do not make the restrained option deliberately weak or assume the more animated one is better.

Present the relevant passage at normal speed with enough surrounding context and sound to judge the change. Use frame stepping and geometry traces to explain defects found in that comparison. A quick browser or viewport preview can support timing decisions while leaving final materials, blur and sound quality unresolved. Carry the user's chosen direction through the final render.

## Check meaningful revision and transfer

When evaluating a production method, change something that might break it: a longer phrase, another value, a different state, a moved cue or a new crop. Inspect the consequences in the output to establish which revisions the method supports. Judge artistic quality separately.

When evaluating the skill itself, use an original brief whose subject or action differs meaningfully from its explanatory example. Give a fresh author the portable guidance and necessary original assets. Keep private reference evaluation separate from the author's inputs when testing standalone transfer. Record any review-assisted changes so a guided revision is not reported as an unassisted success.

Match the test to the claimed capability. A short motion study can diagnose a local construction problem; it cannot validate a skill for complete product films. For that claim, produce a complete argument with enough development to assess product introduction, changes of attention, continuity, pacing, sound and resolution. Let the story determine duration. Keep local studies as production tests within that work rather than repeatedly substituting them for the requested film.

A useful worked example includes the brief, important poses, original construction, actual event timing, sound relationship, output and a revision. State what was rendered, measured, heard and accepted, and label unproduced intentions as proposals. A faithful reconstruction demonstrates correspondence with its source. Directing a new film needs a separate trial.

## Hand back an honest, usable version

Deliver the requested playable film and identify material changes. Summarize checks and remaining limitations in proportion to the task. Distinguish a version ready for review from one the user has accepted, and name any visible creative gap alongside the technical results.

Continue routine authorized production through completion. Use concrete alternatives for a material unresolved taste choice, preserve the selected direction, and make the next revision possible from the saved source and version record.
